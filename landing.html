◈ HISTORY OF THE THEORY
A JOURNEY THROUGH TIME
The Veneziano Formula
Gabriele Veneziano discovers a formula describing hadron scattering amplitudes using Euler's beta function — unknowingly writing the first string theory equation.
ORIGIN
1968
1970
Strings Proposed
Nambu, Nielsen, and Susskind independently interpret Veneziano's formula as describing one-dimensional vibrating strings — the birth of string theory proper.
BREAKTHROUGH
Supersymmetry Enters
Ramond, Neveu, and Schwarz introduce fermionic strings, merging string theory with supersymmetry to create superstring theory — which requires exactly 10 dimensions.
SUPERSTRINGS
1971
1984
First Superstring Revolution
Green and Schwarz prove anomaly cancellation in Type I superstring theory. The physics community floods into string research. Heterotic strings discovered by Gross, Harvey, Martinec & Rohm.
REVOLUTION
Calabi–Yau Compactification
Candelas, Horowitz, Strominger & Witten show that six extra dimensions can be compactified on Calabi–Yau manifolds, connecting string theory to particle physics phenomenology.
GEOMETRY
1985
1995
Second Revolution & M-Theory
Witten's landmark talk at Strings '95 unifies all five superstring theories into 11-dimensional M-theory via duality symmetries. D-branes are identified by Polchinski as non-perturbative objects.
UNIFICATION
AdS/CFT Correspondence
Juan Maldacena conjectures the duality between string theory in Anti-de Sitter space and conformal field theories on its boundary — the most cited paper in theoretical physics.
HOLOGRAPHY
1997
2003
The String Landscape
Flux compactifications produce an astronomically large number of possible string vacua — the landscape. The anthropic principle enters fundamental physics debates.
LANDSCAPE
ER = EPR Conjecture
Maldacena and Susskind propose that Einstein–Rosen bridges (wormholes) are equivalent to Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen pairs (entangled particles), connecting gravity and quantum information.

◈ GLOSSARY
KEY TERMS DEFINED
Worldsheet
The 2D surface swept out by a string as it propagates through spacetime, parameterized by σ (spatial) and τ (time) coordinates. Analogous to the worldline of a point particle.
Calabi–Yau Manifold
A compact complex manifold with vanishing first Chern class. String theory requires 6 extra dimensions to be curled up into one of these intricate geometric shapes at every point in space.
D-Brane
A Dirichlet membrane — an extended object of p spatial dimensions on which open strings are anchored. D-branes carry Ramond-Ramond charge and play a crucial role in non-perturbative string dynamics.
T-Duality
A duality that equates a string theory compactified on a circle of radius R with another theory on a circle of radius α'/R. Small and large radii are physically equivalent — a striking quantum geometry effect.
Regge Trajectory
The linear relationship M² = J/α' + const between a string's angular momentum J and mass squared M². Different vibrational modes of the same string appear as distinct particles on a Regge slope.
Dilaton
A scalar field in string theory whose expectation value determines the string coupling constant gₛ = e^φ. The dilaton controls the strength of string interactions and is related to Newton's constant.
Graviton
The massless spin-2 particle mediating gravity, which emerges naturally from the closed string spectrum as the lowest vibrational mode. This is why string theory inevitably contains a quantum theory of gravity.
Moduli Space
The space of all inequivalent vacuum solutions of string theory. Different points in moduli space correspond to different shapes and sizes of the compactified extra dimensions — and different physics.
Holography
The principle, exemplified by AdS/CFT, that a theory of quantum gravity in a volume of space is fully described by a lower-dimensional theory on its boundary — like information encoded on a 2D surface describing a 3D room.
Tachyon
A hypothetical particle with imaginary mass that appears in bosonic string theory, indicating vacuum instability. Superstring theory naturally eliminates the tachyon via GSO projection, one reason it is preferred.
Compactification
The process of "curling up" extra dimensions into tiny compact spaces so they are undetectable at low energies. The choice of compactification manifold determines the effective 4D physics we observe.
Supersymmetry (SUSY)
A symmetry relating bosons and fermions. Each particle has a "superpartner" with the same mass but opposite statistics. Essential for consistent superstring theories and for solving the hierarchy problem.
◈ DID YOU KNOW
MIND-BENDING FACTS
REALITY IS MUSICEvery particle you've ever interacted with is a different "note" played by a vibrating string. Matter is frozen music at the Planck scale.
TOO SMALL TO SEEStrings are roughly 10⁻³⁵ meters long — 20 orders of magnitude smaller than a proton. We'd need an accelerator the size of the galaxy to probe them directly.
HIDDEN DIMENSIONSSix extra dimensions may be curled up around every point in space. They're so small they haven't been detected, but their shape determines the physics of our universe.
INFINITE UNIVERSESThe string landscape contains roughly 10⁵⁰⁰ possible universes — each with different constants of nature. Finding ours among them is one of physics' deepest puzzles.
BLACK HOLE COUNTINGString theory gave the first microscopic derivation of black hole entropy in 1996 (Strominger & Vafa), counting actual microstates and matching Bekenstein–Hawking exactly.
GRAVITY IS EMERGENTIn AdS/CFT, gravity isn't fundamental — it emerges from quantum entanglement on a lower-dimensional boundary. Space itself may be woven from quantum information.
ACCIDENTAL GRAVITYString theory wasn't invented to explain gravity. It started as a theory of nuclear forces in 1968. The graviton appeared unexpectedly and changed everything.
26 OR 10?Bosonic string theory requires 26 dimensions to be consistent. Adding supersymmetry reduces that to 10. M-theory adds one more, landing at 11 — the magic number.

◈ FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
YOUR QUESTIONS ANSWERED
What exactly is a "string" in string theory? ▾
A string is a one-dimensional object — like an infinitely thin rubber band — vibrating at the Planck scale (~10⁻³⁵ m). Unlike point particles, strings have spatial extent and can vibrate in many modes. Each distinct vibrational pattern corresponds to a different elementary particle: electrons, quarks, photons, gravitons, and more are all the same fundamental object oscillating differently. Open strings have two free endpoints; closed strings form loops.
Why does string theory need extra dimensions? ▾
String theory requires extra dimensions for mathematical consistency — specifically to cancel quantum anomalies that would otherwise make the theory nonsensical. Bosonic string theory needs 26 dimensions; superstring theory needs 10; M-theory needs 11. The six extra spatial dimensions beyond our familiar three are "compactified" — curled up into tiny geometries (Calabi–Yau manifolds) far too small to detect at current energies, likely near the Planck scale.
Is string theory the same as M-theory? ▾
Not quite — M-theory is larger than string theory. There are actually five distinct consistent superstring theories (Type I, Type IIA, Type IIB, Heterotic SO(32), Heterotic E₈×E₈). Edward Witten showed in 1995 that all five are related by dualities and are different limits of a single 11-dimensional theory he called M-theory. What the "M" stands for is deliberately left vague — Witten suggested "magic, mystery, or membrane." M-theory is still not fully formulated.
Has string theory been proven or tested experimentally? ▾
Not directly — and this is one of the main criticisms of string theory. The strings themselves vibrate at the Planck scale, some 15 orders of magnitude beyond what the LHC can probe. However, string theory has made indirect contributions: it led to the precise derivation of black hole entropy (verified within its mathematical framework), inspired the AdS/CFT correspondence which makes testable predictions in strongly-coupled quantum field theories, and predicted supersymmetric particles (SUSY has yet to be observed at the LHC).
What is the string landscape and why is it controversial? ▾
The string landscape refers to the vast number of possible vacuum states in string theory — estimated at 10⁵⁰⁰ or more — each corresponding to a different compactification of extra dimensions with different physical constants. Some physicists see this as a feature: it naturally explains why our universe's constants are finely-tuned for life (anthropic reasoning). Critics argue it makes string theory unpredictive — if any universe is possible, how do we derive ours? It remains one of the most debated aspects of modern theoretical physics.
What is the AdS/CFT correspondence? ▾
The Anti-de Sitter / Conformal Field Theory correspondence, conjectured by Juan Maldacena in 1997, is a remarkable duality: a theory of quantum gravity (string theory) in a negatively-curved (AdS) space is mathematically equivalent to a quantum field theory without gravity on that space's boundary. It's a holographic relationship — the bulk 3D physics is encoded on a 2D surface. This duality has proven enormously useful for studying strongly-coupled quantum systems including quark-gluon plasma and superconductors.
What are D-branes and why do they matter? ▾
D-branes (Dirichlet branes) are extended objects — surfaces — on which the endpoints of open strings are constrained to move. A Dp-brane has p spatial dimensions. Discovered by Joseph Polchinski in 1995, they are non-perturbative objects that carry Ramond-Ramond charge and have their own dynamics. D-branes are crucial for understanding string dualities, constructing realistic particle physics models within string theory, and computing black hole entropy from first principles by counting brane microstates.
How does string theory unify gravity with quantum mechanics? ▾
General relativity describes gravity as spacetime curvature, while quantum mechanics governs the microscopic world. Combining them directly leads to infinities (non-renormalizability). String theory resolves this because strings have finite extent — the UV divergences that plague point-particle quantum gravity are smoothed out. When you quantize a closed string, a massless spin-2 particle (the graviton) automatically appears in the spectrum. String theory doesn't just include gravity — it predicts it, making it the leading candidate for a consistent quantum theory of gravity.
